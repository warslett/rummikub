# Rummikub Official Rules

## Overview

- **Players**: 2–4 (standard); 5–6 with Rummikub XP/Mini XP expansion
- **Ages**: 7 to adult
- **Standard contents**: 106 tiles — 8 sets of tiles numbered 1–13 in four colours, plus 2 joker tiles; 4 racks + rack holders
- **XP/Mini XP contents**: 160 tiles — 12 sets of tiles numbered 1–13 in four colours, plus 4 joker tiles; 6 racks + rack holders

---

## R-01: Object of the Game

Be the first player to play all the tiles from your rack by forming them into sets (runs and/or groups).

---

## Sets

### R-02: Definition of a Group

A group is a set of three or four tiles of the same number, each in a different colour. Colours may not repeat within a group. Since there are only four colours, a group can contain at most four tiles.

Example: `7(red) 7(blue) 7(black)` or `7(red) 7(blue) 7(black) 7(orange)`

Invalid: `7(red) 7(red) 7(black)` (colour repeated)

### R-03: Definition of a Run

A run is a set of three or more consecutive numbers all in the same colour.

Example: `3(blue) 4(blue) 5(blue) 6(blue)`

### R-04: Number 1 is Always Lowest

The number 1 is always played as the lowest number. It cannot follow the number 13 in a run.

---

## Set-Up

### R-05: Tile Distribution

Place the tiles face down on the table and mix them thoroughly. It is recommended to stack the tiles in piles of 7 for ease of playing.

### R-06: Determining First Player

Each player picks a tile; the player with the highest number goes first. Return the tiles to the table and mix them.

### R-07: Initial Rack

Each player takes 14 tiles and places them on their rack.

### R-08: The Pool

The remaining tiles are called the "pool."

### R-09: Games per Round

Each round is made up of multiple games. The number of players determines the number of games in a round:

- 4 players → 4 games per round
- 3 players → 3 games per round
- 2 players → 2 games per round

Players may determine their own number of rounds (house rules permitted).

### R-10: Ending a Game

When a player plays the last tile on their rack, a game ends. Players then start over until they have played the agreed number of games/rounds.

---

## Playing the Game

### R-11: Tile Values

Each tile is worth its face value (the number shown on the tile).

### R-12: Initial Meld Requirement

In order to make an initial meld, each player must place tiles on the table in one or more sets that total at least 30 points. These points must come from the tiles on each player's rack; for their initial meld, players may not use tiles already played on the table.

### R-13: Joker Value in Initial Meld

A joker used in the initial meld scores the value of the tile it represents.

### R-14: Drawing a Tile

When players cannot play any tiles from their racks, or purposely choose not to, they must draw a tile from the pool. After they draw, their turn is over.

### R-15: Turn Order

Play passes to the left (clockwise).

### R-16: Turns After Initial Meld

On turns **after** the turn on which a player made their initial meld (not on the same turn), that player may do any combination of the following:

- Play one or more new sets from their rack onto the table
- Add tiles from their rack to existing sets on the table
- Manipulate existing sets on the table (see Manipulation)

A player may combine all of these actions in a single turn. At least one tile from the player's rack must be played during the turn.

### R-17: Mandatory Draw When No Play

On any turn that a player cannot or chooses not to play a set from their rack, add onto another set, or manipulate existing sets, that player picks a tile from the pool and their turn ends.

### R-18: Cannot Play Just-Drawn Tile

Players cannot lay down a tile they just drew; they must wait until their next turn to play this tile.

### R-19: Winning a Game — "Rummikub!"

Play continues until one player empties their rack and calls "Rummikub!" This ends the game and players tally their points (see Scoring).

### R-20: Game End When Pool Is Empty

If there are no more tiles in the pool but no player has emptied their rack, play continues until no more plays can be made. This ends the game.

---

## Manipulation

### R-21: Manipulation Principle

Players try to table the greatest amount of tiles by rearranging or adding to sets which are already on the table. Sets can be manipulated in many ways as long as at the end of each round only legitimate sets remain and no loose tiles are left over.

### R-22: Adding Tiles to Existing Sets

A player may add one or more tiles from their rack to make a new set from tiles already on the table.

Example: Blue 4-5-6 on table → add blue 3 from rack to extend the run. Add blue 8 to the group of 8s already on the table.

### R-23: Removing a Fourth Tile from a Group

A player may remove a fourth tile from a group and use it to form a new set.

Example: Group of four 4s on table → take one blue 4 and use it with blue 3, 5, 6 from rack to form a run.

### R-24: Adding and Removing from a Set

A player may add a fourth tile to a set and remove one tile from it to make another set.

Example: Run 8-9-10 on table → add blue 11 from rack, then remove the 8s to form a new group.

### R-25: Splitting a Run

A player may split a run by removing a tile and using it to form a new set.

Example: Run 4-5-6-7-8 on table → split by removing the 6 and using it with another tile to form two new runs.

### R-26: Combined Split

A player may combine tiles from multiple existing sets and tiles from their rack to create new valid sets.

Example: Move a tile from a run and a tile from a group together with a rack tile to form a new group.

### R-27: Multiple Split

A player may manipulate three or more existing sets on the table simultaneously, using tiles from their rack to create new valid sets.

### R-28: No Loose Tiles After Manipulation

At the end of a player's turn, all sets on the table must be legitimate (valid groups or runs) and no loose tiles may be left over.

---

## The Joker

### R-29: Joker as Wild Tile

There are two jokers in the game (four in XP edition). Each joker can be used as any tile in a set, and its number and colour are that of the tile needed to complete the set.

### R-30: Retrieving a Joker

On future turns, a joker can be retrieved from a set on the table by a player who can replace it during their turn with any tiles that can keep the set legitimate. This tile can come from the table or from a player's rack.

### R-31: Replacing Joker in a Group of Three

In the case of a group of three tiles, the joker can be replaced by a tile of either of the missing colours.

### R-32: Joker Becomes Wild Again After Retrieval

When a player retrieves a joker, the joker will once again have any value or colour.

### R-33: Must Play Retrieved Joker on Same Turn

A player who retrieves a joker must play the joker on their current turn to make a new set.

### R-34: Must Use a Rack Tile When Retrieving Joker

When retrieving a joker, the player must also use at least one tile from their rack on that turn (just as on any other turn).

### R-35: Cannot Retrieve Joker Before Initial Meld

A player cannot retrieve a joker before they have played their initial meld.

### Four Ways to Clear the Joker:

#### R-36: Replace Joker Directly

Replace the joker in a set with the tile it represents (from rack or table).

#### R-37: Split a Run to Clear Joker

Split a run that contains a joker, rearranging the tiles so the joker is freed.

#### R-38: Add a Tile to Clear Joker

Add a tile to a set containing a joker in such a way that the joker is freed.

#### R-39: Combined Manipulation to Clear Joker

Split and rearrange multiple sets to free the joker (e.g., move tiles from a run to groups, freeing the joker in the process).

---

## Time Limit

### R-40: One Minute Per Turn

There is a time limit of 1 minute per player, per turn. Players who go over the time limit must draw a tile from the pool, ending their turns.

### R-41: Incomplete Runs Penalty

Players who cannot complete a move within the 1 minute time limit must replace the tiles that were on the table to their previous positions, take back the tiles they played, and draw 3 tiles from the pool as a penalty. This ends the turn.

---

## Scoring

### R-42: Losing Players' Score

After a player has cleared their rack and called "Rummikub!", the other players add up the value of the tiles they are holding on their racks as a negative figure.

### R-43: Winner's Score

The winner of the game receives a positive score equal to the total of all the other players' points.

### R-44: Score Verification

The winner's score should equal the total of the other players' scores in each game and at the end of each round (as a check).

### R-45: Joker Penalty on Rack

The penalty for having a joker on a rack is 30 points (counted as negative value).

### R-46: No Winner — Pool Exhausted

In the rare case that all the tiles in the pool are used before any player goes "Rummikub!" and no player can play any more tiles, the player with the lowest value of tiles on their rack wins the round. Each losing player's score equals the winner's rack total minus their own rack total (this results in a negative number for each losing player). The winner receives a positive score equal to the sum of all losers' score changes.

### R-47: Starting a New Game

After scoring the game, players place all the tiles back on the table and begin the next game by following the Set-Up directions.

### R-48: Tracking Wins

In addition to keeping track of points, players keep track of the number of games each player has won.

---

## Winning the Round

### R-49: Round Winner

When the last round ends, the player who has won the most games in all rounds combined is the winner.

### R-50: Tiebreaker

In the case of a tie, the player with the highest score is the winner.

---

## Strategy Notes

- The beginning of a game may seem slow, but as the table builds up, more plays are possible.
- In the early stages, it can be a good idea to hold back some tiles so that other players open up the table and provide more opportunities for play.
- Sometimes it is useful to hold back the fourth tile of a group or run and play only three, so that on the next turn you can play a tile instead of drawing from the pool.
- Keeping a joker on your rack is also a good strategy; however, you risk being caught with it (earning a penalty of 30 points) when another player goes "Rummikub!"
